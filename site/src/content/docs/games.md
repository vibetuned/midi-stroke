The **Games** tab has short games about timing, and they need no instrument: a key, a tap on the
screen or a drum pad will do. They're for anyone, whether or not you play yet. Every level is real
rhythm, and the songs are real pieces you can go on to play.

## Slingshot

Hold to orbit, let go to fling. Press and hold **space** (or touch, or any MIDI key) as the probe
reaches an anchor, and it whips around it, a quarter turn every beat. Let go at the white mark and
it's flung to the next anchor. How long you hold *is* the note: a half note is half a circle, a
whole note a full one, and the note sounds for exactly as long as you hold it. Each anchor shows
the note written out (an open whole or half note, a filled quarter, flagged eighths, dotted
values). The part of the orbit still to go glows up to the mark where you let go, and shrinks as
you hold, so you always know where you're going and where to stop.

- **Lessons** teach one idea each: half notes, quarter notes, whole notes, a waltz in 3/4, eighth
  notes, dotted rhythms.
- **Songs** are the melodies of well-known tunes from the library: *Twinkle Twinkle*, *Ode to Joy*,
  *Scarborough Fair* and more. Finish one and **Play it on the saxophone →** opens it in the saxo
  app, to learn the notes as well as the rhythm.
- **English folk tunes:** *God Save the King*, *Pop Goes the Weasel*, *Ham Frolick*, *Aire de
  l'Opéra françoise* and *Auld Lang Syne*, also from the saxophone library. The clicks follow the
  metre: in 6/8 they count eighths, or dotted quarters when the tune is quick, and a tune that
  starts on an upbeat is counted in up to it.
- **My pieces:** add any MEI score with **📁 Add a piece**, and its top line becomes a level. It's
  kept on your device, and it also appears in the piano app under *My pieces*, where the button
  after a run takes you.
- **Speed:** *Relaxed* plays at three quarters of the tempo; *As written* plays at the piece's own.

After each run you see how it went, with stars, accuracy, and a tip when you tend to let go early
or rush. You also see **the rhythm written out, every note coloured by how you played it**.

## Conductor

Conduct the choir, one beat at a time. Every note of the tune has its own singer, standing low to
high like the keys of a piano. Hold **space** (or touch, or any MIDI key) for one beat: a ring
fills around the singer as the beat goes by, and whoever has a note in that beat sings it. Let go
as the ring closes and press again straight away for the next beat. A long note takes several
holds, and the singer keeps singing through them; two quick notes can sing inside one hold.

The song follows you. Let go too soon and the singer is cut off, surprised. Hold on too long and
they keep the note until they run out of breath, turning red. Wait before the next beat and the
whole choir stops to wait for you. The conductor's baton moves with your beats, and the display
shows the tempo you're conducting at against the piece's.

- **Lessons** are the Slingshot's rhythms, each given a tune, from three singers to eight, with a
  piano playing the chords.
- **Folk songs** from the piano library: *Hänschen klein*, *Summ, summ, summ*, *Kuckuck* and more.
  The choir sings the tune while the piano plays the left hand. **Play it on the piano →** takes
  you to the piece.
- **English folk tunes**, sung a cappella: *God Save the King*, *Auld Lang Syne*, *The Drunken
  Sailor*, *Pop Goes the Weasel*, *Yankee Doodle*, *Camptown Races*, *Ham Frolick*, *The British
  Grenadiers*, *Ronda* and *Aire de l'Opéra françoise*. The quick notes are the choir's to sing:
  you still hold one beat at a time. The slow 6/8 tunes are conducted in six, one hold an eighth,
  and a tune that starts on an upbeat begins with a hold that has the upbeat in its second half.
- **My pieces** work here too.
- **After a run**, a chart shows every beat as you conducted it: taller where you stretched the
  song, lower where you hurried it. You also see how much longer or shorter the song came out,
  and the tune written out, each note coloured by how its beats went.

## Rhythm echo

Sing the second voice of a canon by defending the cities from it: a musical *Missile Command*,
with a good ending. A star sings a line, and each of its notes falls as a bolt of plasma on the
city of its pitch, one city for every note of your voice, low on the left. A satellite watches a
line across the sky, the negative event horizon, and every bolt crosses it just as your voice (a
bar or two behind) should sing that note. Hold **space** (or touch, or any MIDI key) as it crosses
and let go as its tail does: the city's turret fires at it, and you hear your note for as long as
you hold. Defend them all and the two voices together are the canon.

- **Let go too soon**, or never fire, and the rest of the plasma falls on the city: flames rise
  from it.
- **Hold on too long** and the turret overheats: flames rise from the turret.
- **The shield dome** charges as you go, and the last bolts are counted down. When your part is
  done the dome goes up, and the hollow bolts, the star's last notes that your voice doesn't
  answer, burst harmlessly on it. Then the fires go out, the lights come on, and there are
  fireworks.

The results show what happened to every bolt, bar by bar: a green burst when it was intercepted,
flames on a house when it reached its city, flames on a turret when it overheated.

- **Lessons** are short canons, one idea each: long notes, quarters, rests, *Frère Jacques* as a
  round, eighths, dotted rhythms, a waltz, and finally two bars behind.
- **The Kunz canons** (Konrad Max Kunz, *200 short canons*, Op. 14) are real two-voice pieces
  from the piano library. Afterwards, **Play both voices on the piano →**. More are on the way.
- **My pieces**: add a two-voice canon of your own, and the voice that follows is yours.

## Groove Builder

Build a drum groove one part at a time, like a looper. The groove is a wheel of pads, like a game
of Simon: a ring for each part, and a pad for every step of the bar. A bar is counted in, then you
play the kick once round, on the outlined pads, with **space**, a touch, or any MIDI key or pad.
From then on the kick plays exactly as you played it while you add the snare, then the hi-hat, and
so on. After the last part, the whole groove plays twice, and you hear what you built.

Every hit lands on a step, like on a drum machine, and **what you play is what you get**. A hit
on the wrong step stays wrong for the rest of the song, and a missed one stays missing. The step
playing lights up its column of pads as the groove goes round.

- **Grooves** come from the drum library, 24 of them, easiest first: ska, rock, disco, twist,
  pop, rhythm & blues, reggae, funk, bossa nova, Afro-Cuban, cha-cha, a shuffle, swing and a slow
  blues in 12/8, a triplet funk, and a six-part samba.
- **After a run**, the groove is shown as a step sequencer, the same grid as the drums app, with
  every hit on its step, every wrong one, and every one missing. **Play it on the drums →** opens
  the beat in the drums app.

## Sounds

Each game has its own voice. The Slingshot's notes are made from NASA space recordings, through
*Nasa Space Pad*. The Conductor's choir sings with *The Spellsinger*, and Rhythm echo's star and
turrets with *Space Voices*; its plasma lands with the meteoroid strike. The Groove Builder's drum kit is cut from sounds recorded on Mars: the
Perseverance rover's laser zapping rocks, its wheels, its MOXIE machine, and a meteoroid strike
heard by InSight.

## Timing calibration

Every device takes a moment to play a sound and to notice a key. Choose **⏱ Calibrate timing**
once and tap along to eight clicks. The games then take that moment off, so a hit on the beat
counts as on the beat.
