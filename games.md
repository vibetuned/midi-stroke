### **1\. The Symplectic / Phase-Space Shooter ("Resonance Drive")**

Instead of steering a ship in standard 2D Cartesian space $(x, y)$, your controls directly manipulate **momentum ($p$) and position ($q$)** of a driven harmonic oscillator.

* **The Core Physics**: A standard oscillator conserves phase space area via symplectic integration ($H \= \\frac{p^2}{2m} \+ \\frac{1}{2}kq^2$). In phase space, an unperturbed orbit is an ellipse or circle.  
* **The Rhythm Input**: Taps on the beat act as **parametric kicks** (like pumping your legs on a swing at the resonant frequency).  
  * Hitting on the beat injects energy, cleanly expanding your orbital amplitude or snapping your phase angle ($\\theta$) by $90^\\circ$ or $180^\\circ$.  
  * Tapping off-beat distorts the orbit into chaotic Lissajous figures or introduces phase drift, dragging you out of the safe resonant pocket.  
* **The Threat**: Enemies don't just shoot bullets at you; they form **standing wave patterns, nodal lines, and energy barriers**.  
  * Some barriers can only be breached at peak kinetic energy (maximum momentum $p$, position $q \= 0$).  
  * Others require you to hover at the turning points (zero velocity $p \= 0$, maximum potential $q$).  
* **Why it works**: You aren't dodging obstacles manually. You are "tuning" a physical vibration to match the tempo and meter of the track.

### **2\. The Brass Virtuoso ("Lip Slur Panic")**

*Trombone Champ* works because a slide is continuous, smooth, and easily mapped to mouse Y-axis motion. A trumpet, however, is fundamentally different—and mechanically much weirder.  
A trumpet produces pitch through two separate systems: **the harmonic overtone series** (controlled by your lips/embouchure) and **three piston valves** (which alter the tube length to fill in the chromatic gaps).

* **The Control Scheme**:  
  * **Right Hand (Keyboard keys J, K, L or gamepad triggers)**: The 3 valves. There are only 7 valve combinations ($0$, $2$, $1$, $1+2$, $2+3$, $1+3$, $1+2+3$), each dropping pitch by a set number of semitones.  
  * **Left Hand (Mouse vertical position / Mouse wheel / Pitch stick)**: Embouchure tightness. This selects the target harmonic register (Pedal C, low G, middle C, E, G, $B\\flat$, high C).  
* **The Expressive Comedy**:  
  * On a trumpet, the overtone registers get closer together the higher you play. In your game, the higher up the register you blow, the narrower the vertical sweet spot becomes.  
  * If your lip position is slightly misaligned, you don't just miss a note: you **"crack" the note**, violently honking the overtone immediately above or below it.  
* **Auxiliary Mechanics**:  
  * **Spit Valve**: Moisture builds up over time, muffling the audio with wet gargling. You must hit a quick purge key during multi-bar rests.  
  * **Mutes & Wah**: A key or mouse-click to insert a straight mute, cup mute, or slap a plunger mute over the bell for syncopated "wah-wah" jazz solos.

### **3\. The Inertial Slingshot ("Centripetal Groove")**

Rhythm game inputs usually check position or timing *at impact*. This concept checks timing *at release*.

* **The Mechanic**: You pilot an tethered probe orbiting planetary anchors in space.  
* **The Loop**: Holding a button latches your tether to the nearest celestial gravity node, causing you to whip around it in an arc. Releasing on the downbeat flings you tangentially into the next orbit corridor.  
* **Rhythmic Poly-meters**:  
  * A $4/4$ track might give you a node where holding for exactly two beats rotates you $180^\\circ$ toward the next path.  
  * A waltz ($3/4$) or syncopated track forces you to hold for odd fractions of a rotation, flinging yourself across intersecting hazard lanes.  
* **Why it works**: It replaces twitch-reflex button tapping with a continuous sensation of momentum, tension, and release.

### **4\. The Clockwork Assembler (Micro-Precision Crafting)**

Instead of abstract tracks, every song is an intricate, rapidly running machine (a mechanical chronometer, an antique music box, or a safe lock) that must be built in real time.

* **The Mechanic**: Gear teeth, escapement levers, balance springs, and pins feed down interlocking tracks.  
* **The Loop**: Each beat requires a specific mechanical action:  
  * **Quarter notes**: Dropping heavy gears onto rotating axels.  
  * **Eighth notes**: Setting fine escapement clicks or tensioning springs.  
  * **Sustains**: Cranking a winding stem or holding an abrasive wheel against a jewel bearing.  
* **The Fail State**: Messing up doesn't drop a combo meter. It introduces mechanical friction: gears strip, springs snap across the screen, and the backing track dynamically desynchronizes, warping pitch and grinding the tempo down.
